About Age Bridge Initiative

ABI is a human capital and institutional development programme with three parts: Mentorship, Professional Orientation and Career Development, and Institutional and Organizational Development.

The problem

Poverty persists, especially for women, youth and vulnerable groups.

Causes include weak governance, institutions that do not respond to people's needs, youth unemployment, conflict, climate disasters, food shortages, and limited access to education, health, productive assets and social services.

Aid and results

The aid that reaches lower-income countries is out of proportion to the results it produces.

Two reasons are given. Institutions have weak capacity to absorb resources. And there are too few skilled people to deliver results on time.

Our approach

ABI sets out to improve the performance of people, especially youth, and of institutions. It unlocks existing skills and expertise, strengthens institutional capacity, and connects generations.

It takes a systems approach to sharing knowledge between generations. It is grounded in Results-Based Management and informed by the right to education.

Experts from many sectors

ABI draws on professionals from government, the United Nations system (serving and retired), the private sector, academia, civil society and faith-based organizations, at national, regional and international levels.

Four working principles

  • Goal-oriented

    Every pairing sets SMART goals: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.

  • Evidence-based matching

    Mentors and mentees are matched on skills, experience, personality and career goals.

  • Reciprocal learning

    Mentors guide, and mentees bring fresh perspectives.

  • Trust-centered support

    A safe, confidential space for problem-solving and honest feedback.

Who takes part

  • Mentors

    Experienced professionals from government, the UN system, the private sector, academia, civil society and faith-based organizations.

  • Mentees

    Students, early-career professionals and young job seekers.

  • Organizations

    A Corporate Mentoring Programme exists, with an agreement covering the mentor, the mentee and the organization. It is developmental and not tied to performance appraisal.
